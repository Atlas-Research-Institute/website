We introduce research to students who have never had a way in.

Research is how knowledge gets made. Students in well-resourced schools meet it early, through programs, mentors and teachers who have done it themselves. Students in much of South Asia, Central Asia and Africa usually do not.

Atlas gives those students a first chance to run their own project, for free, and to learn how research actually works by doing it.

The summer 2026 fellowship

About 500 students applied in two weeks. We admitted 25 fellows from 20 countries, and they wrote research papers.

88% of the fellows we surveyed had never finished an independent research project before Atlas.

94% said, at the end of the fellowship, that they plan to keep doing research.

How Atlas works

  1. Open to everyone who applies

    The Atlas community

    Recorded lessons, research guides and other opportunities students can apply to.

  2. For admitted fellows

    The Atlas Fellowship

    One theme and a fixed start and end date. Each fellow finishes a first research paper with weekly lessons and feedback.

  3. For fellows who finish

    Research groups

    Small groups, each run by a student lead, that take the work further and write papers for submission.

Partners and journals

Where fellows' work goes, and who supports them.

  • International Journal of High School Research
    Peer-reviewed submission route for fellows' papers.
  • The Curieux Review
    Student research writing partner.
  • Lumiere Education
    Program partner. Scholarships to Lumiere programs for Atlas fellows.
  • The Atlas Journal
    Where fellows share finished work. Read by Atlas editors before it goes up, not externally peer reviewed.
